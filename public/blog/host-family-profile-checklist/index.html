How to write a clear host family profile

AuPairUp · Draft reviewed against linked sources · 2026-10-21

Help an au pair understand everyday life in your home. Specific details make it easier for both sides to recognise a potential fit.

Describe a normal week

Start with a short introduction to your household, the languages you speak and the interests you share. Then describe a typical week. Explain when you would need help, which adults are usually at home and how you organise changes.

Avoid broad phrases such as «help whenever needed». Give examples of the tasks you expect and the tasks you do not expect. Distinguish your preferences from legal conditions: the applicable rules depend on your country and the participant's circumstances. Check those rules before presenting an offer.

  • Use age ranges rather than children's full names or dates of birth.
  • Describe the general area and access to transport without publishing your street address.
  • Explain the routine, expected tasks, time off and arrangements for changes.

Explain what living together would feel like

An au pair needs to understand the home as a place to live, learn and rest. Describe the proposed room, shared spaces, meals and household habits. Make room for their questions about privacy and independence.

Explain how language learning and local activities could fit into the routine. Be honest about transport: a rural home with limited buses creates different practical needs from a city flat. If driving is relevant, discuss experience and check the applicable licensing and insurance conditions separately.

Be specific about expectations

List what each side would need to discuss before deciding: proposed financial arrangements, responsibilities for expenses, communication habits and how you would handle disagreements. Do not treat a warm introduction as agreement on these details.

A useful profile also explains what matters to your family without describing a «perfect person». For example, «we eat together most evenings and would like to discuss how that fits your preferences» starts a conversation. «You must become part of the family immediately» leaves much more room for misunderstanding.

Keep private information for the right stage

Do not publish children's photographs, school details, identification documents or a precise address in an introductory profile. Decide together how to exchange references and other information through appropriate channels.

Before sharing a profile, read it from an au pair's perspective. Could someone describe your normal week after reading it? Could they tell which questions still need discussion? If not, replace an adjective with an example. A clear profile is an invitation to talk, not a promise of a successful arrangement.
